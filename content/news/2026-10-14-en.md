**The conclusion first:** On the early morning of October 14 (KST), Google released its new top model, Gemini 4 Argon, worldwide. On Google's own table it leads 12 of 18 benchmarks, but on the independent Artificial Analysis index it ties GPT-6 Astra (53) and sits below Claude Opus 5.5 (58). The differences that stand out are the hallucination rate (15%) and output that can run to 1M tokens in one response.

## When it shipped, and what shipped

- **Announcement:** October 1, 2026, 05:00 KST. At first it was offered only to defensive security organizations through the Fairwind program. ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/))
- **General release:** October 14, 2026, 01:00 KST. It rolled out to paid Gemini API tiers, AI Studio, Google Antigravity (Ultra subscribers), and the Gemini app and Search AI Mode (Pro and Ultra subscribers). ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/))
- **Model ID:** `gemini-4-argon`. The default thinking level is medium, and low, medium, or high can be selected. ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/latest-model))

## Main capabilities

- **Input, thinking, and output share one 1,048,576-token window.** A single response can pass 64K and run up to 1M tokens (Long decode). ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/latest-model))
- **In Antigravity it can be selected as the model for running `/goal` and for coordinating multiple agents.** ([AI Studio developer guide](https://aistudio.google.com/learn/gemini-4-argon-developer-guide)) How `/goal` judges inside a harness is written up in [Claude Code vs Codex /goal](/posts/agentic/claude-code-codex-goal).
- **Tuned for defensive security.** It was trained to find, verify, and patch vulnerabilities, and trusted defensive organizations get it without the cyber guardrails. ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/))
- **Google's internal examples (Google's announcement):** a C/C++ to Rust migration, a libgav1 decoder 2.7x faster than the previous Rust port, and more than 300 TiB of data-center memory saved.

## Price

| | Through Nov 30 (introductory) | From Dec 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input (1M tokens) | $2 | $4 |
| Cached input (1M tokens) | $0.10 | $0.20 |
| Output, including thinking tokens (1M tokens) | $10 | $20 |

It is not available on the free tier or on the Google AI Pro plan in the API. ([Gemini API docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/latest-model))

## Comparison with other models

### Independent evaluation (Artificial Analysis)

![Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index](/news-img/aa-index-en.png)

| | Gemini 4 Argon | GPT-6 Astra | Claude Opus 5.5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intelligence Index | 53 | 53 | 58 |
| Hallucination rate (AA-Omniscience, lower is better) | 15% | 51% | unreported |
| Accuracy (AA-Omniscience) | 50% | 63% | unreported |

Sources: [AT Pulse](https://at-pulse.com/stories/1987/), [endue briefing](https://endue.ai/blog/gemini-4-argon-briefing/) (both cite Artificial Analysis numbers). One analysis says the low hallucination rate comes from answering "I don't know" more often, not from getting more answers right.

### Benchmarks Google published

![Google-published benchmarks](/news-img/benchmarks-en.png)

| Benchmark | Gemini 4 Argon | GPT-6 Astra | Claude Opus 5.5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DeepSWE v1.1 (long-horizon software work) | 77.9% | 74.1% | 74.2% |
| LVBench (long-video understanding) | 91.7% | 87.5% | 83.7% |
| FrontierSWE v2 | 55.0% | 65.5% | unreported |
| Terminal-bench 4.0 | 57.4% | unreported | 66.4% |

Source: [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-unveils-gemini-4-argon-retaking-benchmark-lead-over-openai-and-anthropic-but-in-limited-release), which reproduces Google's published table. Across the 18 items Google published, Argon was the sole leader on 12 and tied for first on 1. "Unreported" means the sources checked here do not give a number. No figures that compare Argon directly with Grok turned up.

### Work automation and security benchmarks

| Benchmark | Gemini 4 Argon | GPT-6 Astra | Claude Opus 5.5 | Grok 4.8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harvey Legal Agent Benchmark (legal research, higher is better) | 19.6% | 5.4% | 3.8% | unreported |
| AutomationBench (Zapier task execution, higher is better) | 51.3% | 41.4% | 42.5% | unreported |
| Gray Swan indirect prompt-injection attack success rate (lower is better) | 0.7% | 8.5% | 1.0% | 51.8% |

Source: [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-unveils-gemini-4-argon-retaking-benchmark-lead-over-openai-and-anthropic-but-in-limited-release), reproducing Google's published material. The Gray Swan figure is the rate at which attacks succeeded, so lower means stronger defense.

## Community reaction

- **Hacker News:** The launch thread reached 1,579 points and 1,048 comments by October 1. The most common complaint was "nobody can use it yet," followed by doubts about the benchmarks. ([endue briefing](https://endue.ai/blog/gemini-4-argon-briefing/), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49914236))
- **Split takes on HN:** One view was that intelligence and cost per task are almost the same as Opus 5.5 High. Another was that it is disappointing. ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49914236))
- **Reddit r/singularity:** The 15% hallucination rate drew the most interest. At the same time, people pointed out that the cost advantage Artificial Analysis cited uses the introductory price, and that the regular price is higher than Astra. ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/singularity/comments/1wug9tb/google_gemini_4_scores_same_as_gpt_6_astra_on/))
- **Skepticism inside Google:** Bloomberg reported that some employees doubt the real coding performance, and Google disputed that report. ([AT Pulse](https://at-pulse.com/stories/1987/))
