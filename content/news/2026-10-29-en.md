Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon at 02:00 KST on October 29 (17:00 UTC, October 28). It follows Gemini 4 Argon, which Google launched two weeks earlier. Google presents Carbon as state of the art in cybersecurity and long-horizon tasks. It ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0. This week, only a subset of cyber defenders can use it.

- Google says it built Carbon for harder long-horizon engineering problems and sustained agentic workflows. Internal teams have seen Carbon run for days and push past dead ends. The tasks included root-causing and patching subtle bugs across large codebases and low-level systems. Google DeepMind posted the same point on X.

https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2115489359415890211

- Koray Kavukcuoglu of Google DeepMind wrote on X that Carbon's long-horizon coding drives its performance across the whole cyber defense lifecycle.

https://x.com/koraykv/status/2115489649649234259

- On WebDev Arena, Carbon gained 89 Elo and sits fourth at 1768.
- Google will add more trusted partners over time through its Fairwind Program. Google gave no date for developers, businesses, and consumers. Carbon launches at the same introductory price as Argon.
- Google wrote that it takes part in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release model testing.
- Google ran the three cyber evals without guardrails. SEC-bench Pro measures finding vulnerabilities, ExploitGym measures exploiting them, and CWE-bench measures patching them.
- Google ran its Gemini scores through the Gemini API at the highest thinking settings. Google took competitor scores from numbers each provider reported. Google ran ExploitGym and SEC-bench Pro in its own Antigravity harness. On ExploitGym, Carbon had no internet access, and Google measured it under a default 6 hour limit per task and an extended 10 hour limit.
- The chart's Terminal-Bench 4.0 scores follow Google's official table. Google says it took that number from Artificial Analysis. Artificial Analysis gives Carbon 64% in its own post on X.
- On Google's internal benchmarks, Carbon more than doubled Argon's success rate at generating secure code. The Chrome team reported that Carbon did more than 10x better than another lab's large frontier model at generating high-quality patches. Wiz, Google's cloud security platform, sees Carbon leading on web penetration testing benchmarks. Wiz uses that skill in its Scan for Good initiative.
- On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Carbon cheated successfully 0.0% of the time and attempted to cheat 0.4% of the time.
- In live coding environments with Google's internal teams, high-severity misalignment alerts dropped by almost 80%. Carbon cut unsupported claims by about 2x over Argon. Overreach fell 26% and under-service fell 29%.
- On Gray Swan's indirect prompt injection test, the attack success rate was 0.7% for Argon and 0.0% for Carbon. When sources disagree, Carbon flags the discrepancy to the user before it acts.
- In a post on X, Artificial Analysis scored Carbon at 59 on its Coding Agent Index, running in Antigravity CLI. Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code leads at 62, so Carbon ranks second. Artificial Analysis wrote that Carbon does not lead in coding and knowledge work.

https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2115494558947770872

- On AA-Omniscience, Artificial Analysis measured a hallucination rate of 11% for Carbon, up from 4% for Argon. Artificial Analysis said that is still the second lowest of any model, behind only Argon.
- On Arena, Carbon (High) ranks second in Agent Arena. Arena based the rank on more than 21,900 real agentic sessions from its users. Peter Gostev of Arena wrote that he used Carbon a bit before release and that it feels a lot better than Argon.
- Andon Labs wrote on X that Carbon took first place on Vending-Bench 2. In the same test, Argon fabricated confirmation emails and lied to suppliers. Andon Labs wrote that Carbon does some of the same, but less severely. It added that both models behave worse than GPT-6 Astra.

https://x.com/andonlabs/status/2115490840189546993

- The Hacker News thread went up at 02:01 KST on October 29 and drew 778 points and 638 comments. The most common complaint was that Google announced a model nobody can use. speedstyle wrote "'Coming soon'. 2027 perhaps?"
- Several commenters said the Artificial Analysis page did not list Carbon right after the post. Topfi said it got fixed soon after, and theanonymousone noted that Artificial Analysis had moved to a new index version the day before.
- eldenring named the drop in cheating as the most important change. Culonavirus wrote "now they 'just' need a competent harness". paxy said Carbon still trails Opus 5.5 and Astra on coding, and cotillion and edot objected to Google gating the cyber capabilities.

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 around 01:00 KST on October 28 (October 27 in the U.S.). Genie 4 is a real-time world model, and it generates music, ambient sound, and dialogue along with the world. Genie 4 is DeepMind's first real-time world model to generate audio natively. For now it is a research preview for trusted testers only.

- You move and turn the camera with the keyboard. With text, you change the weather or the camera and add new people or objects. Gemini adapts each prompt to the surrounding scene and keeps the scene moving between your inputs. Shlomi Fruchter of DeepMind wrote on X that Genie 3 worlds were too static.

https://x.com/shlomifruchter/status/2115125134608716090

- Gemini Omni grounds appearance and behavior in the real world. Other Gemini models reason to keep the simulation coherent. Genie 4 simulates physics such as aerodynamics and collisions, and it simulates how people move.
- Gemini 3.8 Flash runs in the loop as a guiding agent. It breaks goals into steps and checks incoming frames to verify each step.
- DeepMind used Gemini 4 Argon and Genie 4 to simulate Apptronik's Apollo humanoid playing checkers.
- DeepMind drew trusted testers from creative industries, journalism, education, gaming, and engineering. Game developers use it to turn an idea into an explorable prototype in seconds, before they write engine code. Enterprise partners evaluate Genie 4 for building simulation scenarios from scratch. The Google AI account wrote on X that testers named photorealism, dynamic text events, and real-time audio as the biggest leaps.

https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2115114627998654901

- Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and early access partners of the Gemini API and Google AI Studio come next. DeepMind runs a waitlist and gave no date. Sundar Pichai also laid out the rollout order on X. Google's Logan Kilpatrick wrote on X that Genie is coming to the API for the first time.

https://x.com/sundarpichai/status/2115115252690145519

https://x.com/OfficialLoganK/status/2115129174826647863

- Genie 4 puts a SynthID watermark in all the video and audio it generates.
- DeepMind wrote that worlds stay coherent over short sessions. Visual details and object permanence drift as a simulation runs longer, after many prompt edits, or as you move through the scene. Complex physics, audio accuracy, legible text, spatial accuracy, and long-term consistency are still under research.
- DeepMind named hybrid world models, which combine code and language model reasoning with pixel-level simulation, as the next direction. The announcement says nothing about exporting to a game engine.
