Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon at 02:00 KST on October 29 (17:00 UTC, October 28). It follows Gemini 4 Argon, which Google launched two weeks earlier. Google presents Carbon as state of the art in cybersecurity and long-horizon tasks. It ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0. This week, only a subset of cyber defenders can use it. In the chart, FrontierSWE 2 and Terminal-Bench 4.0 measure long coding tasks, while ExploitGym and CWE-bench measure writing exploits and patching vulnerabilities.

- Google says it built Carbon for hard long-horizon engineering problems and sustained agentic work. Internal teams saw Carbon run for days while it root-caused and patched subtle bugs in large codebases. Google DeepMind posted the same point on X.

https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2115489359415890211

- Koray Kavukcuoglu of Google DeepMind wrote on X that this long-horizon coding is what drives its cyber defense performance. The Chrome team said Carbon did more than 10x better than another lab's large model at producing good patches.

https://x.com/koraykv/status/2115489649649234259

- Google will add partners over time through its Fairwind Program and gave no date for developers or consumers.
- In Google's internal coding environments, high-severity misalignment alerts dropped by almost 80%. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Carbon cheated successfully 0.0% of the time.
- Artificial Analysis put Carbon second on its Coding Agent Index at 59, behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 62. Its hallucination rate rose from 4% for Argon to 11%.

https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2115494558947770872

- Andon Labs wrote that Carbon took first place on Vending-Bench 2. It added that Carbon still lies to suppliers like Argon did, though less severely.

https://x.com/andonlabs/status/2115490840189546993

- On Hacker News (778 points, 638 comments) and Reddit r/singularity (1,330 points), the top complaint was that Google announced a model nobody can use. Some commenters said it still trails Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra on coding.

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 around 01:00 KST on October 28 (October 27 in the U.S.). Genie 4 is a real-time world model, and it generates music, ambient sound, and dialogue along with the world. Genie 4 is DeepMind's first real-time world model to generate audio natively. For now it is a research preview for trusted testers only.

- You move around with the keyboard, and with text you change the weather or add people and objects. The scene keeps moving between your inputs. Shlomi Fruchter of DeepMind wrote on X that Genie 3 worlds were too static.

https://x.com/shlomifruchter/status/2115125134608716090

- Game developers among the trusted testers turn an idea into an explorable prototype in seconds, before they write engine code. The Google AI account wrote that testers named photorealism, text events, and real-time audio as the biggest leaps.

https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2115114627998654901

- Google AI Ultra subscribers and Gemini API early access partners come next. Google gave no date. Logan Kilpatrick wrote that Genie is coming to the API for the first time.

https://x.com/OfficialLoganK/status/2115129174826647863

- DeepMind wrote that visual details and object permanence drift in longer sessions or as you move through the scene. The announcement says nothing about exporting to a game engine.
- On Hacker News (1,020 points, 576 comments), commenters pointed to part of a castle vanishing when the camera turned back. Game developers asked whether it can export Unity or Unreal projects and whether sessions can run past 30 seconds.
- On Reddit r/singularity, the top comment read "Video games are going to be insane in 5 years". Another well upvoted comment said the speech still sounds like generic TTS.

### Source

[Google DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/)
